Project Background

Understanding the reality of campus accessibility.

More than 30,000 students with disabilities navigate Chinese university campuses every day — yet physical infrastructure alone cannot guarantee safe, independent movement.

01
Current Situation

The gap between hardware and real experience.

Thousands of students with mobility impairments enter higher‑education campuses every year. Many universities have completed basic accessibility hardware — ramps, elevators, restrooms. But hardware alone doesn't guarantee access.

Research data shows unassisted wheelchair users travel at only 0.67 m/s. They struggle to navigate curb ramps when slopes exceed 1:5, and cannot judge whether a route is passable before arriving on‑site.

National special‑education policy for 2026‑2030 encourages universities to advance digitalised, inclusive campus accessibility construction — turning hardware facilities into usable, real‑life inclusive experiences.

02
Three Core Problems

Barriers that hardware cannot solve.

These persistent challenges limit independence and create spatial inequality across campus.

Dynamic, invisible hazards

Static campus maps fail to capture temporary barriers — construction zones, blocked walkways, parked shared‑bikes and sudden elevator breakdowns. These unrecorded dynamic hazards raise travel risks and reduce independence for mobility‑impaired students.

Broken feedback loops

No unified closed‑loop reporting‑maintenance workflow exists. Students cannot easily submit barrier reports or track whether issues are inspected, repaired or resolved. Faulty facilities may remain broken for weeks with no progress feedback.

Fragmented spatial design

Uneven campus spatial design creates persistent obstacles. Fragmented accessible routes create spatial inequality. Many facilities suffer the "built‑but‑hard‑to‑use" issue — hardware exists on paper yet cannot serve students in daily reality.

Existing general‑purpose navigation software cannot fully satisfy the fine‑grained, campus‑specific accessibility requirements of university students.

— Market Analysis, AccessReady Research
03
Deep Dive

Why the status quo isn't working.

The daily reality for students

Imagine starting each day not knowing whether your usual route to class is actually passable. An elevator that worked yesterday might be broken today. A construction barrier could block the only accessible ramp.

For students with mobility impairments, this isn't hypothetical — it's a daily experience that creates anxiety, dependence, and spatial inequality across campus.

Wasted Time

Students arrive late or get stranded due to impassable routes discovered only on arrival.

Lost Independence

Reliance on classmates or volunteers undermines autonomy and self‑determination.

Market Opportunity

A clear moment for change.

Driven by national inclusive‑education policy and the growing demand for smart‑campus digital transformation, the timing to build a campus‑specific closed‑loop accessibility platform has never been better.

Policy Momentum

The 14th Five‑Year Plan calls for digitalised, inclusive special education support through 2030.

University Needs

Institutions need digital tools to fulfil regulatory obligations and break down internal information silos.

Student Demand

Students urgently need real‑time, user‑centred accessibility information that general navigation software cannot provide.
