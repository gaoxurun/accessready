  We are personally invested in this campus accessibility project
  because we understand the persistent burden facing students navigating
  uncertain campus access. Having witnessed these barriers firsthand, we
  recognize the need for clearer facility status updates and unified
  reporting channels. Our mission is to reduce reliance on repeated
  help-seeking and build a coordinated workflow validated by students
  with mobility limitations.

# The industry and market

## Industry 

\[Which industry will you operate in? Describe the industry -- is it an
emerging or mature industry?\]

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  CampusAble operates within the smart‑campus software industry, a broad
  and diversified market under professional scientific and technical
  services. This industry centres on digital campus management and
  accessibility workflow solutions for tertiary education, with steady
  annual market growth driven by government accessibility mandates and
  digital‑campus upgrades.(Henan Provincial Department of Education,
  2026). While the broader smart‑campus software market is relatively
  mature, the niche market for end‑to‑end contracted university
  accessibility services remains an emerging fast‑growing segment.
  Intense competitive pressure exists in the wider market from
  established campus‑system vendors and general‑purpose digital‑map
  platforms. Successful players in this space rely on compliant
  institutional‑grade integration, verified on‑site data workflows, and
  stable stakeholder cooperation with university administrations. Key
  future industry trends include higher accessibility compliance
  requirements, deep integration between mapping software and
  institutional administrative systems, and service‑oriented contracting
  for campus equity needs.

## Pricing strategy

\[How you set prices for our products/services?\]

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **1. Cost‑oriented pricing**                                          |
|                                                                       |
| The business fully accounts for expenses including platform           |
| development, deployment, cloud servers, operation‑maintenance and     |
| marketing to set base prices with reasonable profit margins. At       |
| launch, CampusAble proposes an annual campus‑subscription fee of      |
| CNY 60 000 plus a one‑off implementation charge of CNY 20 000. This   |
| price reflects our cost structure and supports financial              |
| sustainability. For multi‑campus long‑term clients, instead of direct |
| price discounts, we provide supplementary accessibility analysis      |
| reports as returns. This stabilises revenue while delivering tangible |
| value for universities prioritising campus inclusion.                 |
|                                                                       |
| **2. Value‑based pricing**                                            |
|                                                                       |
| This pricing model captures more than pure subscription fees; it      |
| reflects the comprehensive value universities gain by building        |
| accessible campuses and fulfilling equal‑education policy goals. Each |
| CampusAble subscription improves barrier‑reporting closed‑loop        |
| workflows and reduces accessibility‑management compliance risks.      |
| Compared with generic map competitors, CampusAble is not merely       |
| navigation software but a management service helping universities     |
| meet regulatory obligations. Universities pay for measurable          |
| campus‑inclusion outcomes.                                            |
|                                                                       |
| **3. Psychological pricing**                                          |
|                                                                       |
| For institutional clients, we shape proposals around                  |
| procurement‑decision‑makers' value perceptions. We do not use         |
| consumer‑focused tricks such as psychological odd‑number pricing.     |
| Instead, we offer long‑term partnership benefits and supplementary    |
| reports to reinforce positive perceptions of return‑on‑investment. We |
| fully disclose required on‑site university‑staff workloads to prevent |
| underestimation of total‑cost‑of‑ownership and support realistic      |
| stakeholder expectations                                              |

**Appendix C: Cash Shortfall and Financing Terms**

![](media/image6.png){width="6.266666666666667in" height="3.6625in"}

Based on the twelve‑month cash‑flow forecast for 2027, three cash
scenarios are analysed and plotted on the trend chart.

Scenario 2: Customer receipts delayed by three months Three scheduled
customer payments of CNY 80 000, originally due in July, September and
November, are deferred to October, December and February 2028. Only two
deferred inflows are received within 2027. The year‑end closing cash
balance reaches CNY 288 000, with a minimum monthly closing balance of
CNY 101 000 in September. All monthly cash positions stay above the
CNY 90 000 warning threshold, so no expenditure suspension is required
in the calendar year.

Scenario 3: Founder‑only funding without angel investment If the project
is funded only by the founders' CNY 120 000 equity contribution and the
CNY 300 000 angel investment is forgone, the year‑end cash balance falls
to ‑CNY 12 000. The lowest cash balance of ‑CNY 140 000 occurs in June.
Cash turns negative from March onwards, and non‑essential spending and
new campus expansion must be suspended starting in March.

Baseline reference: normal customer receipts, ending cash CNY 288 000,
minimum closing balance CNY 160 000 in June.
